# Naruto 5e — Locked Mechanical References

**Status:** LOCKED REFERENCE

This file carries forward the reusable mechanical principles that were locked during earlier Naruto 5e play. Campaign-specific story state is intentionally excluded.

## Resolution loop — LOCKED

Every meaningful uncertain action follows:

1. Player declares intended action.
2. GM/adjudicator declares the mechanical resolution: action cost, roll/check/save, target, range and resource cost.
3. Player rolls the required die/dice.
4. GM/adjudicator resolves the mechanical consequence.
5. Only then is the cinematic result narrated.

Unassigned piles of d20 results are not automatically separate checks.

## Action economy — LOCKED

A cinematic description does not create extra actions.

Unless a feature explicitly allows otherwise, a turn has:
- Movement
- 1 Action
- 1 Bonus Action if available
- 1 Reaction per round
- normal free interaction/speech

Hand seals, speed, camera cuts and simultaneous-looking effects are narrative unless represented mechanically.

## Persistent / prepared effects — LOCKED PRINCIPLE

A jutsu may create a persistent or prepared effect when its mechanics permit it. A later turn may exploit that existing effect without recreating the original setup.

Preparation and exploitation remain separate actions/resources unless a specific technique explicitly combines them.

## Cinematic freedom

Once mechanics are resolved, narration can remain highly cinematic. The restriction is on mechanical consequences, not prose style.

## Continuity rule — LOCKED

A historical fictional event can remain canon even when its original mechanics were provisional.

If a later rules decision changes how an old action works mechanically, the historical event still happened; only the reusable mechanical interpretation changes.

## No silent invention

When a required rule is unresolved, mark it **DRAFT**, **PROVISIONAL**, or **UNKNOWN** as appropriate. Do not silently invent permanent modifiers, DCs, damage values, resource costs or extra actions.

These references are intentionally separated from story mode and character-specific campaign state.
