# Naruto 5e — Core Mechanics v0.2

**Status:** ESTABLISHED PLAYTEST RULES

## Core resources

Chakra, Stamina and HP are separate resources. Character-specific values belong in the character profile, not in the shared rules.

## Chakra

Chakra is a numerical resource. It is the actual available Chakra pool, not six d20s.

The earlier `6d20` concept is retired as the core resource model. d20s are resolution dice, not units of Chakra.

Every meaningful jutsu has a Chakra Cost. Subtract that cost from the current pool.

Typical cost guidance:
- Basic: 0–1
- Simple: 2–4
- Moderate: 5–8
- Advanced: 9–14
- Major: 15–20+
- Exceptional: individually determined

These are guidelines, not fixed spell prices.

## Resolution

The d20 resolves uncertainty: attacks, checks, contests, perception and jutsu execution as appropriate.

A jutsu's DC is determined when it is declared, using a stable ladder:

- DC 8 trivial
- DC 10 easy
- DC 12 routine combat technique
- DC 14 demanding
- DC 16 difficult
- DC 18 very difficult
- DC 20 exceptional
- DC 22+ extraordinary

The final DC remains contextual rather than being generated by a rigid universal formula. Factors include complexity, scale, range, duration, precision, simultaneous effects, battlefield interaction, affinity, proficiency and prior preparation.

## Affinity and proficiency

Affinity and proficiency modify resolution rather than automatically creating extra rolls.

Matching elemental affinity is favorable. Non-native manipulation is harder or unavailable according to established abilities.

Known/trained techniques gain familiarity benefits. Improvised techniques do not automatically gain proficiency benefits and may have increased complexity.

Do not roll separate dice for every factor unless a special rule explicitly requires that.

## Control

Control measures chakra-molding precision. Control is a character score, not a flat d20 bonus.

When a Control check is required, derive the Control modifier using the standard 5e ability-modifier progression:

`Control Modifier = floor((CONTROL - 10) / 2)`

Examples:
- CONTROL 7 → −2
- CONTROL 10 → +0
- CONTROL 14 → +2
- CONTROL 16 → +3
- CONTROL 18 → +4
- CONTROL 20 → +5

The check is:

**d20 + Control Modifier vs the declared DC.**

Simple techniques may require no Control check. Complex or improvised techniques may require a Control check against the jutsu DC.

Control does not automatically apply to every jutsu. The GM must declare whether Control is relevant before the roll.

## Action economy

Mandatory procedure:

**DECLARE → MECHANICS → ROLL → RESOLVE → CINEMATIC**

The GM identifies action economy, Chakra Cost, Complexity and DC before the roll. Cinematic narration cannot retroactively compress multiple actions into one turn.

Prepared effects can persist and be exploited later. Setup and exploitation are separate actions/resources unless a specific technique explicitly combines them.

## Stamina and HP

Stamina represents physical endurance/exertion and is separate from Chakra. HP remains the physical survival resource.

## Saves and core scores

Do not introduce the six standard D&D ability scores as Naruto 5e character stats. Use the established Naruto 5e scores contextually, including DEF, Stamina, Control, Chakra and ATK where appropriate.

Not every situation needs a saving throw.

## Nature Energy / Senjutsu

Nature Energy is separate from ordinary Chakra. It can be gathered, stored, transferred, molded with Chakra, or lost through poor control.

Exact Senjutsu subsystem values and limits remain unresolved unless explicitly locked elsewhere.

## Overload

If current Chakra is insufficient for a declared technique, it is normally unavailable. A future Overload mechanic may allow forcing a technique beyond the safe reserve. Until formally established, negative Chakra is not automatically allowed.

## Deliberately unresolved

Affinity bonuses/penalties, proficiency implementation, critical rules, Chakra recovery, Overload, Senjutsu conversion, and level-by-level Chakra progression remain PLAYTEST/DRAFT unless a newer shared rule explicitly establishes them.

Do not silently invent these during play. If necessary, establish a provisional rule and record it for review.
